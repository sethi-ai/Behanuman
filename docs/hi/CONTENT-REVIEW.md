# h.i - Hanuman Intelligence: seed story cards

Editorial working draft · 17 October 2026

These are newly written English editorial paraphrases, not scripture quotations, literal translations, divine speech, or scholarly validation. Reflections and optional questions are contemporary editorial additions. They offer room for interpretation, not prescribed solutions, diagnoses, or promises of outcomes. A scenario such as feeling overwhelmed by interview preparation is an example of a reader's circumstances, not a diagnosis or a statement that the ancient passage addresses interviews.

All three cards deliberately use the Valmiki Ramayana online text identified below. They do not blend in Ramcharitmanas details. Sundara Kanda is a section of this work; Sundarkand is also a section of Tulsidas's distinct Ramcharitmanas. These cards are not labeled Puranic stories.

## 1. A strength named aloud

ID: hi-jambavan-valmiki-4-66

### Story · Original editorial paraphrase

The vanara company was discouraged. Hanuman sat quietly apart, while Jambavan turned his attention toward him. Instead of speaking only about the distance to be crossed, Jambavan spoke about the one who might cross it.

He recalled Hanuman's power, intelligence, courage, and connection to Vayu. He told the story of Hanuman's childhood flight toward the sun, and of the blessings he had received. His words placed the task before Hanuman alongside a remembrance of who Hanuman was.

Jambavan urged him to rise and cross the ocean. The others were waiting to witness what he could do. Stirred by this appeal, Hanuman enlarged his form, bringing joy to the company. Before the leap itself, the narrative gives us this exchange: one companion speaks, another responds, and a discouraged gathering finds reason for hope.

### A reflection · Editorial

Strength is not shown here in isolation. Another person's recognition belongs to the moment too.

### Optional question

What stays with you about Jambavan's part in this story?

## 2. The smaller form

ID: hi-surasa-valmiki-5-1

### Story · Original editorial paraphrase

During Hanuman's journey over the ocean, the heavenly beings asked Surasa to put an obstacle in his way. They wished to see how he would meet the encounter. Taking a fearsome form, she confronted him and demanded that he enter her mouth.

Hanuman explained the purpose of his journey in Rama's service. But Surasa would not let him pass. As she opened her mouth wider, Hanuman made his body larger. The encounter grew in scale, each enlargement answered by another.

Then Hanuman changed his response. He became as small as a thumb, entered her mouth, and came straight out. Offering his respects, he declared that her condition had been fulfilled and that he would continue toward Sita. Surasa returned to her own form and wished him success in reuniting Sita with Rama. Hanuman continued his journey.

### A reflection · Editorial

The change in scale is striking: the same Hanuman who grows enormous also knows how to become small.

### Optional question

Which moment in this encounter draws your attention?

## 3. Before the first words

ID: hi-speaking-to-sita-valmiki-5-30

### Story · Original editorial paraphrase

Hanuman had found Sita, but finding her did not settle how he should approach her. Hidden among the branches, he considered her distress and the dangers around her. He wanted to offer reassurance without exposing her to further fear.

He thought about his appearance and his manner of speaking. A strange figure speaking unexpectedly might seem like another deception by Ravana. An alarm could bring the guards, endanger Sita, and place the mission at risk. Remaining silent also troubled him: he did not want to leave her without comfort or return without speaking to her.

Hanuman considered how his words might reach her gently. He decided to begin with Rama, whose memory already held her attention. From the shelter of the branches, he would speak of Rama's qualities and deeds in a gentle voice, seeking to make his message believable rather than startling.

### A reflection · Editorial

His wish to help includes attention to the person receiving the message, not only to the message itself.

### Optional question

What do you notice in Hanuman's deliberation before he speaks?
